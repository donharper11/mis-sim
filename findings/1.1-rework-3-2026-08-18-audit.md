### 1.1-r3-006 — `options: [on, off]` — the most natural switch vocabulary there is — is rejected as `E00 "Unreadable pack · restore or repair policies.yaml"`, and the authoring doc does not warn — BUILD (docs) · 1.2 (message)

`loader.py` uses `yaml.safe_load`, which is YAML 1.1: `on`, `off`, `yes`, `no`, `true`, `false`
are **booleans**, not strings. A policy switch is precisely the place an author writes them.

```
on/off vocabulary        yaml -> {'options': [True, False, 'audit'], 'default': False}
                         REJECTED ['Input should be a valid string' ×3]
yes/no vocabulary        yaml -> {'options': [True, False], 'default': False}
                         REJECTED ['Input should be a valid string' ×3]
quoted on/off            yaml -> {'options': ['on', 'off'], 'default': 'off'}
                         ACCEPTED options=['on', 'off'] default='off'
```

It fails loudly rather than silently, which is the right failure — but it fails through the `E00`
path of `1.1-r3-002`, so the instructor is told to *"restore or repair `policies.yaml`"* when the
actual fix is two quote marks. `docs/casepack-schema.md`'s new `options` section is otherwise the
best-written part of this branch and says nothing about it.

**Fix:** one line in the new `options` and `default` section — *"quote any option that YAML reads
as a boolean: `on`, `off`, `yes`, `no`, `true`, `false`"* — plus `1.1-r3-002`'s message work in
1.2. Cheap, and it is the first thing a pack author will hit.

### On the packet

Two things deserve saying plainly.

The first is that **this packet did the hard part of a two-field change correctly**, and the hard
part is not the constraint — it is proving that a field which is declared is also a field which is
*read*. A schema addition that parses, validates clean, and quietly discards its input is
indistinguishable from a working one at the validator's output, and it would have sailed through a
DoD table built entirely from exit codes. The builder saw that, said so, and inspected the loaded
object. Everything downstream of 1.1 now rests on that check rather than on a green run.

The second is about the substitution. The deviation was right, the packet was small, the harm was
zero, and it would have been entirely reasonable to wave it through — which is exactly why it is
worth filing. What propagated to `main` was not a wrong branch base; it was a habit of settling a
question by asserting what a document says instead of opening it. That habit is the one thing this
project's three protocols are jointly built to prevent, and it got past a builder and an author in
the same afternoon, in a packet too small for anyone to look twice at. `rework-3.md §7` calls the
branch-base slip *"the sixth instance of an instruction naming a route that could not be taken as
written."* The seventh instance is in the sentence that records it.
