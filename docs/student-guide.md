# MIS Simulation — student guide

Updated 2026-10-06 for the current five-stage navigation. Your instructor supplies the
site address, student login and round deadlines. Demo credentials are for local rehearsals
only and are listed separately in `demo-accounts.md`.

## Your task

Manage the inherited IT estate of Riverside Grocers over six rounds. Technology creates
business value only when people can use it and management makes coherent decisions.
Your results combine Technology, Organisation and Management; buying more software alone
does not guarantee a better result.

## Start a round

Sign in with **Student ID** and **Password**. If asked, select your class section.
Confirm the team and round in the header. Open **Dashboard** and **Challenges** to read
what happened and what needs attention. Before the first advance there are no round results.
If your team has not been assigned or initialized, ask your instructor to prepare it.

Decisions are shared by your team. Coordinate saves: another member may change the sheet
while you are editing. If a save reports that the round changed, reload before continuing.

## Work through your decisions

1. **Strategy:** choose the competitive approach your team intends to follow and save it.
   The current interface locks a declared strategy after round 2. Explain your choice in
   business terms: cost, customers, relationships or the needs of a focused segment.
2. **IT Infrastructure:** inspect the inherited services and the infrastructure, security
   and people controls. Host platforms group your services and applications for planning.
   A platform container by itself does not purchase a service. Review each actual purchase's
   setup cost, recurring cost and lead time before provisioning it.
3. **Applications:** inspect existing applications and in-flight projects. When purchasing,
   choose the deployment/configuration and consider additional cost categories. Check the
   project status: an order with a lead time is not automatically available this round.
4. **Rollout & Adoption:** open each application's tab. Choose training, process change and
   communication, then **Save Changes**. The displayed costs belong to the selected options;
   these are not arbitrary spending amounts. **Reset** restores the saved choices. Use
   **Ownership** to assign the relevant owners and sponsors. Communication
   is shared at the business-unit level, so it can affect more than one application's tab.
5. **Review & Budget:** read the **Decision Sheet** and **Budget & Capital** tabs. Compare
   capital and ongoing costs; inspect any warnings. A capital request follows the authored
   limits and approval rounds; a justification is not an automatic approval.

Use **Challenges** to select an available response and rationale. A decision note records
why you acted. It is not an AI-generated score. Revisit Review after changing a choice.

## Lock and review the outcome

When the whole team agrees, click **Lock round** in Review. Editing closes until the round
advances or the instructor reopens it. Warnings are teaching information; they do not all
prevent you from locking. A deadline or instructor action may lock the sheet for you.

After advancement, open **Debrief**. Read the scorecard, changes, events, prevented events,
financial result and capability explanations together. Separate what you bought from what
arrived, what people adopted and what actually contributed value. **Download report** saves
a text copy. Use the next round to respond to this evidence, not just the headline score.

## Discuss with your team

- What business outcome were we trying to improve, and who was affected?
- Did we provide the training, process changes and ownership needed to use the technology?
- What recurring commitments and delayed effects did we create?
- Which warning did we act on or ignore? What evidence connects it to the outcome?
- What did the result teach us about the strategy we chose?
